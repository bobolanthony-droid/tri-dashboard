    Training load by sport

    Fitness, fatigue and form

    Fitness (CTL) – your 42-day average daily training load. It rises slowly as you build; this is the number to grow toward race day.
    Fatigue (ATL) – your 7-day average daily load. It reacts fast to hard weeks and drops fast with rest.
    Form (TSB) = fitness − fatigue. Negative means you are carrying fatigue from training (normal in build weeks; below −30 is a lot). Around 0 to +15 on race day means fresh.
    Ramp flag – a week where fitness rises by 5 or more. That is a steep build; sustained, it raises injury and illness risk.

        Swim

        "Swimming lengths" keeps only lengths you actually swam; lengths more than 35% slower than the session's median length (wall rests folded in by the watch) and rest intervals are removed. Open-water swims have no wall rests, so the two values differ only by pauses (moving vs elapsed time).

        Aerobic drift (power:HR decoupling)

        Drift: how much HR rose relative to power from the first to the second half; under 5% indicates good aerobic durability for that duration. Only rides with both power and HR appear. Bike efficiency (EF) and power at Z2 heart rate are in Heart-rate trends.

        Aerobic efficiency by sport (your first 3 sessions = 100)

        Higher = more speed or power per heartbeat. Run and swim: metres per heartbeat (swim uses swimming lengths only, rests removed). Wrist HR in the pool is less reliable, so read swim trends loosely.

        Like-for-like: speed at your Z2 heart rate

        Average pace or power over the seconds you spent in your Z2 band (run 141–149 bpm, bike 135–149 bpm, from your intervals.icu zones), skipping the first 5 minutes. Only sessions with 5+ minutes in the band count. It rises only when you are actually fitter, whatever the session type.
